# Automated CASL evidence qualification (policy `casl-auto-v3`)

Status: **built and tested; live authorization is OFF** (`CrmLeadAutomation.autoCaslActive = false`). Product controls, not legal advice. Activation needs a legal review of this document and one explicit owner decision (see *Activation*).

**v3 (launch-readiness audit, 2026-10-18)** changes two things only. (1) The restriction scan gained vendor / representative / solicitor wording ("no agents or vendors", "sales representatives, please do not call", "solicitors will be prosecuted"), "do not email us about SEO/services", withheld-consent wording, and French "offres de service non sollicitées … refusées", "ne doit pas être utilisée à des fins de sollicitation", "représentants … priés de ne pas" (each was demonstrated to pass v2 undetected; false positives only ever skip an address). (2) Commercial sends now require full sender identification (`senderIdentificationGaps`): a legal name, a plausible street-style mailing address (letters and a number, not a placeholder) and at least one more contact channel (e-mail, phone or https web address). Previously only a non-empty mailing address was checked. Policy version `casl-auto-v2` → `casl-auto-v3`: any owner activation of v2 is void (none exists).

**v2** aligns the engine with an independent legal-research report (October 17, 2026): identity corroboration is separated from publication; customer-service mailboxes no longer qualify by name; personal/free-mail addresses are excluded; each SUPPORTED decision stores a reproducible evidence artifact; terms/policy pages are part of the restriction check; evidence is good for at most **30 days** and is re-checked at **every send**; automated mail to Québec businesses must be in French. Policy version `casl-auto-v1` → `casl-auto-v2` (any owner activation of v1 is void; none exists in production).

## 1. Why this exists
GarageOS wants a clean pool of Canadian independent repair shops it may lawfully email, with **zero routine per-contact human approvals** and **zero unjustified automated authorizations**. PR #159 had added a mandatory second-person approval; that was an internal application policy, not a statutory CASL requirement, and it has been withdrawn (section 6). It is replaced by deterministic, evidence-based, auditable rules that skip every recipient about whom there is reasonable doubt.

## 2. Research — authorities, and what we could and could not verify
**Retrieval limit (material):** the build environment could not reach `laws-lois.justice.gc.ca`, `crtc.gc.ca`, `decisions.fca-caf.gc.ca`, `canlii.org`, `priv.gc.ca`, `legisquebec.gouv.qc.ca` or `oqlf.gouv.qc.ca` (DNS/proxy refusals). Everything below was taken from search-result summaries and law-firm commentary, whose quotation marks are **not verified verbatim**. Counsel must read the primary texts before activation.

| Authority | What it is reported to say (unverified paraphrase) | How the engine uses it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| CASL **s.6** | A CEM may be sent only with consent (express or implied) and in the prescribed form: identification, contact information, unsubscribe mechanism | Footer/identification/unsubscribe already enforced at send (`footerLinesFor`, `NO_UNSUBSCRIBE_CONFIG`) |
| CASL **s.10(9)(b)** | Implied consent where the recipient **conspicuously published, or caused to be published,** the address, the publication is **not accompanied by a statement** that they do not wish to receive unsolicited CEMs, and the message **relates to their business, role, functions or duties** | The three legal elements → `publishedByRecipient`, `noRestriction`, `relevant` |
| CASL **s.11** | Unsubscribe requests to be honoured without delay and in any case within 10 business days | Suppression checked at every send (`activeSuppressions`) |
| CASL **s.13** | The **onus of proving consent is on the person who alleges it** | Everything is recorded as evidence; nothing is inferred |
| CRTC official guidance / FAQs, Bulletin 2012-548 | Conspicuous publication is a higher standard than public availability; a third party's reproduction of an address on its own initiative does not count; records (date, address, URL, screenshots) should be kept | Own-site only; artifact with the publication context |
| CRTC **2016-428** (Blackstone Learning) | Reliance on s.10(9)(b) failed where the sender could not show where/how/when addresses were found, whether publication was conspicuous, or that no restriction accompanied it | Per-element evidence + artifact; no provenance shortcuts |
| CRTC **2017-368** (Compu-Finder) | The burden rests on the sender; "general public availability … coupled with assumptions or speculation as to what the functions of the receiving organization or individual might be" is not enough; generic/central addresses (e.g. info@) were among those sent to; an unsubscribe link that errors is a violation | Relevance needs page evidence, not a guess from the local part; generic mailboxes need a shown business-wide context |
| **2020 FCA 103** (3510395 Canada Inc. / Compu-Finder) | Upheld the constitutionality of CASL and the CRTC's finding that the exception was not met; many addresses came from third-party directories or carried disclaimers | Directories/third parties never count; disclaimers BLOCK |
| **PIPEDA** s.4.01 (business contact information) and s.7.1 (collection/use of electronic addresses by program) | s.4.01 reportedly removes business contact information used solely to communicate about the person's employment/business/profession from Part 1; s.7.1 restricts collecting addresses with a program designed to generate, search for and collect them (exact exceptions **not retrieved**); the OPC has treated harvested business e-mail addresses as personal information (PIPEDA 2016-003, reported). The research agent could not confirm section numbering | The crawler never searches for addresses: it verifies the **one** address already on file against the shop's own site and never builds, guesses or generates addresses; no profiling or enrichment |
| **Québec** Act respecting the protection of personal information in the private sector, art. 1 (Law 25) | Reportedly excludes from the Act's sections II and III personal information about the exercise of a function in an enterprise (name, title, work address, work e-mail, work phone) | Only business contact information of a business is handled; personal addresses are excluded by policy |
| **Québec** Charter of the French language ss.52, 58 (Bill 96) | Commercial documents (s.52) and commercial advertising (s.58) must be in French (bilingual only on conditions). **No retrieved source states whether a B2B solicitation e-mail falls under them** | GarageOS policy: automated mail to a Québec business is French-only (see 4.7); counsel/OQLF to confirm the legal reach |
| Overture Maps Places schema | `sources[]` per-property attribution (`property, dataset, license, record_id, update_time, confidence`); no `provider` field; data licence (CDLA Permissive 2.0) is not consent | Provenance recorded for audit, never evidence of publication |

Conclusions that drive the design: public availability, a dataset's licence, or a third-party database proves **nothing** about the recipient's publication or consent; a dataset is an aggregator, not the original publisher; the sender must be able to **show** each element for each address and **at the time of sending**; therefore only the shop's **own website** can be machine-verified, and everything else is skipped.

### Legal mandates vs deliberately conservative GarageOS policy
| Rule | Legal mandate? | GarageOS policy choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recipient published (or caused to be published) the address | **Yes** — s.10(9)(b) | Proven only for an address on the shop's **own** site and **own** domain, in a deliberate contact placement (mailto link or contact context), **not obfuscated**, with the site's identity corroborated |
| No accompanying no-solicitation statement | **Yes** | Every page read **and every linked terms/legal page** must be read and clean; an unread page is never assumed clean |
| Message relates to business/role/functions | **Yes** | Contextual proof per mailbox class; customer-service mailboxes need a shown business/management function |
| Sender bears the onus | **Yes** — s.13 | Reproducible, hash-verified evidence artifact per SUPPORTED decision |
| Unsubscribe / identification | **Yes** — ss.6, 11 | Unchanged; checked at every send |
| Two independent signals for identity | No | Policy |
| Own-domain addresses only; free-mail/personal excluded | No (the law is not limited to this) | Policy — the personal-data and attribution risk is not worth the volume |
| Obfuscated (Cloudflare/`[at]`) addresses never count | No | Policy — not "conspicuous"; decoding defeats the site's anti-harvest protection |
| 30-day maximum evidence age, 14-day revalidation, re-check at every send | No fixed statutory period | Policy — onus is at the time of each send |
| French-only automated mail to Québec businesses | Unclear (see above) | Policy |

## 4. The conditions and how each is proven (rule set `casl-auto-v3`)
An address is **SUPPORTED** only if all six recorded facts are MET (`src/domain/lead-radar/casl-evidence-engine.ts`). Identity and publication are **separate facts**.
1. **identityCorroborated** *(policy)* — the website on the prospect record is really this business's site: at least **two** of {the business's phone, its postal code, its name} appear on the pages read. This is identity **only**; it never makes publication MET by itself.
2. **publishedByRecipient** *(legal element, conservative proof)* — MET only when identity is MET **and** the address is on a page of the same site (registrable-host match; directories, social pages and site-builder subdomains never count) **and** the address's domain **is** the site's domain **and** it is placed as a deliberate contact (a `mailto:` link, or text in a contact/enquiry context) **and** it is not obfuscated (Cloudflare e-mail protection and `[at]/[dot]` forms are INSUFFICIENT: `OBFUSCATED_PUBLICATION`). Otherwise UNKNOWN ⇒ INSUFFICIENT.
3. **noRestriction** *(legal element)* — no statement against unsolicited / commercial / marketing / vendor messages (English + French patterns, deliberately broad) on any page read ⇒ otherwise **BLOCKED**; and the check must be **complete**: the publication page read in full (not cut at the size cap), and **every terms/legal/disclaimer/anti-spam page linked from the pages read** read (same site only; up to 3; an off-site, unreachable, robots-blocked or over-budget terms page counts as unread). Privacy pages are read when on the same site and the budget allows but are not required. Incomplete ⇒ UNKNOWN ⇒ INSUFFICIENT (`RESTRICTION_CHECK_INCOMPLETE`).
4. **relevant** *(legal element, policy proof)* — by mailbox class: **GENERAL_CHANNEL** (`info@ contact@ hello@ office@ admin@ …`) only when the page presents it as the business-wide contact; **MANAGEMENT** (`owner@ direction@ gerant@ …`) by role; **FUNCTION_SPECIFIC** (`service@ appointments@ booking@ rdv@ sales@ reception@ support@ …`) **only when the page describes the mailbox as a channel for business/management matters** (otherwise `MAILBOX_FUNCTION_MISMATCH`); **NAMED** only when the page ties the person to owning/managing the business; **IRRELEVANT** (HR, privacy, legal, billing, noreply, press…) ⇒ **BLOCKED** without spending a request. The class and the message topics it is relevant to (`SHOP_MANAGEMENT_SOFTWARE`) are stored on the basis and re-checked at each send.
5. **attributable** *(policy)* — the address is on the business's own domain. Personal/free-mail addresses (Gmail, Hotmail, Yahoo, Videotron, Bell…) are **BLOCKED** (`PERSONAL_ADDRESS_EXCLUDED`) before any request, even when the shop's site shows one; earlier lawful CRM evidence for such an address is untouched.
6. **documented** — the reproducible evidence artifact (section 4.1) was captured.

Outcomes: **SUPPORTED** (all MET) · **BLOCKED** (restriction, suppression/DNC, irrelevant role, personal address) · **INSUFFICIENT** (everything else). `INSUFFICIENT` and `BLOCKED` never authorise anything; there is no "ask a human" outcome.

### 4.1 Evidence artifact (`CrmCaslAssessment.snapshot`)
Stored for SUPPORTED decisions and for restriction BLOCKs only (nothing is stored for INSUFFICIENT — nothing was proven). Contents: schema/rule version, address, assessment time; the **publication** (final URL, host, page kind, HTTP status, observation time, how it was published — mailto/text —, page `<title>`, a ≤1,200-char sanitised HTML fragment around the address, a ≤800-char visible-text context with the address replaced by `[address]`, content hash, byte count, completeness); identity signals; the **restriction scan** (every page read: URL, kind, hash, bytes, completeness, restriction found?; the offending sentence + page for a BLOCK; unread-policy count); the **robots.txt outcome** per host; the mailbox class and why it is relevant; known limits (`STATIC_HTML_ONLY`, `SAME_SITE_PAGES_ONLY`, `NO_CLAIM_AFTER_OBSERVATION`). Other people's e-mail addresses are redacted (`[other-address]`); the whole page is never stored; the artifact is < 8 KB. `snapshotHash` is a key-order-independent SHA-256 over the artifact so an audit can prove it was not altered; **Lead Radar → Automation → Latest assessments → (address)** shows the artifact and verifies its hash (Super Admin, read-only). SQL for auditors: `SELECT id, "emailNormalized", outcome, "mailboxClass", "observedAt", "expiresAt", "snapshotHash", snapshot FROM garageos."CrmCaslAssessment" ORDER BY "createdAt" DESC;`.

### 4.2 Freshness, and the check at every send
* Evidence is good for **30 days** from the *earliest* page read in the decision (`expiresAt`); the stage re-reads at **14 days**; INSUFFICIENT retries at 7 → 30 → 90 days; BLOCKED is re-read after 90 days; a changed website or a new rule version re-opens an address at once. A SUPPORTED re-validation on a new day is a new history row with a fresh artifact.
* **Every send** re-evaluates (`evaluateAutomationBasis`, called by the composer policy, the sequence runner, the dispatcher and the eligibility engine, each time): policy activated for this exact version → the observation is ≤ 30 days old (also if `expiresAt` were missing) → the recipient address **equals** the assessed address → the message's subject is covered by the basis's relevant topics (automated Lead Radar mail carries `SHOP_MANAGEMENT_SOFTWARE`; a person's own message declares none, so an automated basis does not cover it) → suppression, bounce, complaint, objection, DNC, unsubscribe configuration, language and the daily cap (`evaluateSendPolicy`) → for a Québec recipient the message language is French.
* **Not** re-fetched per send: the website itself (network budget). A restriction added to a site inside the ≤ 14-day revalidation window is therefore not seen until the next read — see *Known limits*.

### 4.3 Québec
`territoryDefaultLanguage` keeps the FR default for Québec (and EN elsewhere). New: an **automated** commercial message to a Québec recipient (province `QC`, or a Québec postal area when there is no province) is **blocked unless written in French** (`QUEBEC_FRENCH_REQUIRED`); an unknown language fails closed. The built-in French templates are test-checked to contain no English wording; the French footer carries the legal name, mailing address, contact details, the reason for receiving the message and "Se désabonner". Manual messages by a person are not constrained by this rule.

## 5. What can and cannot be verified automatically
Automatic: that an address is on the business's own site and domain; how it is placed; that no restriction statement is on the pages read, including the linked terms; mailbox class; identity corroboration by phone/postal/name; suppression/DNC/duplicates; freshness; robots.txt compliance; recipient/topic/language at send time.
Not provable by machine (hence skipped, never guessed): that the person who controls the site is the person who owns the mailbox; that a page we did not or could not read has no restriction (off-site terms, JavaScript-rendered or over-size pages); anything on a social network or directory (never read); consent that exists only in a human's memory; whether a generic mailbox is really the right addressee of a particular pitch (policy proxy only); and — above all — **that CASL is satisfied**, which is a legal conclusion and the reason live authorization is a separate owner decision.
Uncertain records are skipped without human intervention: they simply stay INSUFFICIENT and are retried on the schedule above; sellers are never asked to research them.

## 6. Removal of the two-reviewer requirement
Removed: `approveSendingBasis` / `rejectSendingBasis`, the review page and list, `reviewAuthority`/`canApproveEvidence`/`canRejectEvidence`, `APPROVAL_REQUIRED_KINDS`, `ALLOW_SUPER_ADMIN_SELF_REVIEW`, the `approve_casl_evidence` capability, same-person independence (`person.ts`, `setPersonKey`, the Team "same-person links" panel), the pending-review queue, `PENDING_REVIEW` as a produced status and readiness state. A basis a person records still needs structured evidence (completeness is checked by rule; incomplete records are refused); a complete one is stored `AUTO_SUPPORTED`.
Kept (history is never rewritten): every existing `APPROVED / REJECTED / PENDING_REVIEW / LEGACY_UNREVIEWED / NOT_REQUIRED` row, `reviewedBy*`, audit events, and the `User.personKey` column (unused). A historic `APPROVED` basis is still honoured by the strict automation reading and is never replaced by a newer automated row. A historic `PENDING_REVIEW` row (never approved) authorises nothing and reads as *pending evidence*.

## 7. Safety boundary
* `AUTO_SUPPORTED` is valid only when `autoCaslActive` is true **and** `autoCaslRuleVersion` equals the current `CASL_RULE_VERSION` — for every send path (composer, enrollment, sequence worker, dispatcher, eligibility), including manual sends. Any rule change silently returns to "not activated".
* Before activation, a SUPPORTED contact is **EVIDENCE_SUPPORTED** (readiness), counted and visible, never READY and never send-authorized.
* Nothing in this pipeline enrols, schedules, sends or touches a send switch/cap; the evidence stage has its own switch (`evidenceEnabled`) beneath the automation master switch.

## 8. Activation (owner action, after legal review)
1. Counsel reviews sections 2–5 against the primary CRTC texts and signs off the rule set (or asks for changes → bump `CASL_RULE_VERSION`).
2. Review a sample of real SUPPORTED assessments (each has a stored, hash-verified evidence artifact — open it from the Automation page) and the aggregate counts on *Lead Radar → Automation*.
3. A Super Admin types the confirmation phrase in *Automation → Automated CASL policy → Activate*. The action is audited (`AUTO_CASL_POLICY_ACTIVATED`); deactivation is one click and immediate.
4. Activation changes no send switch. Sending, the pilot, sequences and every cap remain separately OFF until the owner turns them on.

## 9. Operating cost
Per candidate address: ≤ 6 pages + robots.txt(es) = **≤ 9 requests**, ≤ 400 KB per page, 6 s per request, 15 s wall, one attempt per request, concurrency 4, 25 candidates per tick (every 15 min) ⇒ ≤ 2,400 candidates/day; terms/policy pages are fetched **only after the address is found**; typical shop: robots + 2–4 small pages (≈ 0.3–0.6 MB). Free-mail and irrelevant-role addresses cost **zero** requests. Storage: one history row per decision, with a < 8 KB artifact only for SUPPORTED/restriction decisions; no raw HTML is kept. No AI is used anywhere in the engine.

## 10. Known limits (material)
* The primary legal texts could not be retrieved in the build environment (section 2): counsel must confirm every paraphrase above.
* DNS-rebinding window between the address check and the connection; JavaScript-rendered pages are not executed (INSUFFICIENT); off-site terms/policies cannot be read (INSUFFICIENT); only English/French restriction patterns; a restriction added after the last read is not seen until the next revalidation (≤ 14 days) — mitigated by the 30-day ceiling and the per-send checks, not eliminated.
* The Overture-sourced address list itself may have been collected by a program (PIPEDA s.7.1); GarageOS only verifies, never collects, and cannot prove the dataset's collection method — counsel to decide whether that is acceptable.
* Whether an automated B2B e-mail must be French under the Charter, and whether a generic mailbox is the correct addressee of a software pitch, are legal questions this system answers conservatively, not authoritatively.
* A franchise or multi-business site (shared domain) is guarded only by the independent-repair ICP gate; personal data in a named mailbox's context is limited to the ≤800-char artifact context.

## 11. Recovery of INSUFFICIENT results (acquisition version 2)
Measured on production (4,285 INSUFFICIENT rows): EMAIL_NOT_FOUND_ON_OWN_SITE 34.7 %, FETCH_FAILED 29.4 % (86 % of them recorded as `http://`; 43 % never reached a request), no usable website 11 %, relevance-policy reasons ~14 %, RESTRICTION_CHECK_INCOMPLETE 5.9 %, BUDGET_EXHAUSTED 3.5 %, OBFUSCATED 4 %, ROBOTS 1.6 %. Transient reasons are a SUBSET of INSUFFICIENT (1,663 rows); first-pass pending and parked rows are not INSUFFICIENT.
Changes (how pages are FOUND and READ; the six legal/policy conditions of §4 are untouched, `CASL_RULE_VERSION` stays `casl-auto-v3`): the same site's `www` / other-scheme form is tried when the recorded form does not answer at all; contact pages are discovered by link text as well as URL, then by four conventional URLs; per-candidate ceiling 8 pages / 12 requests (was 6 / 9); a malformed `%` sequence in a mailto no longer aborts an assessment. Deliberately NOT changed: obfuscated addresses, free-mail, named/function-specific mailbox relevance, "info@ must be shown as the contact channel", off-site terms pages, robots.txt.
`ACQUISITION_VERSION` (stored in each assessment's `usage.acq`) lets an INSUFFICIENT result made by an older crawler, for a reason a better crawler can change, be re-opened exactly once; the new result is appended to the history. Read-only measurement: `GET /api/webhooks/cron/lead-radar?diag=evidence` and `?diag=recovery&since=<ISO>` (cron-authenticated, counts only; workflow "Lead Radar evidence diagnostic").
